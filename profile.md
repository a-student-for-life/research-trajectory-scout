# Research Taste Profile

## Core Identity

My research identity is:

> **Machine learning on underexplored sensing modalities in real-world, resource-constrained environments.**

This is the sentence every paper recommendation is scored against. It should not change unless my research direction genuinely changes.

---

## Research Shape (what to match — not topics, shape)

I am strongest in applied machine learning for sensing and signal-processing problems,
especially where the data source is unusual, underused, noisy, indirect, or difficult to collect.

I prefer topics where the sensing modality itself matters:
audio, satellite imagery, AIS/GPS traces, RF/WiFi signals, vibration,
thermal imaging, low-cost IoT sensors, smartphone sensors, environmental sensors,
industrial sensors, or multimodal combinations.

I am drawn to problems where:
- there is a real-world monitoring, detection, classification, segmentation, or anomaly-detection task;
- the dataset is scarce, geographically underrepresented, noisy, or fragmented;
- a useful contribution can come from building a pipeline, benchmark, dataset, or deployable system;
- the work can be completed with student-level resources in 8–12 weeks;
- the topic has public-sector, environmental, infrastructure, transportation,
  disaster-response, maritime, urban, agricultural, or safety relevance.

---

## Proven Project Pattern

My best ideas have this structure:

1. A neglected real-world problem
2. A non-obvious data source or sensing modality
3. A sensing or signal-processing challenge
4. A practical ML model (not necessarily novel — applied skillfully)
5. A full pipeline (preprocessing → model → evaluation), not just one model
6. A publishable evaluation with clear metrics
7. A possible dataset contribution
8. Feasibility within student-level resources

## Desired Paper Types (in priority order)

1. Dataset + benchmark papers
2. Applied ML systems papers with real-world evaluation
3. Domain-specific method papers (sensing + ML + evaluation)
4. Edge AI / TinyML sensing papers
5. Real-world deployment papers

Deprioritise:
- Conceptual / position papers
- Survey papers (unless highly targeted)
- HCI studies
- LLM prompt-behavior papers
- Benchmark-chasing papers on saturated datasets

---

## Strong Fit Areas

- Underexplored sensing modalities
- Real-world data collection from underserved geographies
- ML pipelines using practical datasets
- Anomaly detection on sensor data
- Classification or segmentation on sensor/satellite data
- Edge AI or resource-constrained deployment
- Remote sensing, acoustic sensing, RF sensing, thermal sensing, vibration sensing, multimodal
- Dataset and benchmark creation
- Domain adaptation for noisy real-world signals
- Public-sector or infrastructure-related applications
- Indian or Global South context papers (genuine gap, not just novelty framing)

---

## Weak Fit Areas (reject these)

- Speculative HCI systems
- Non-linguistic intent or thought-to-AI interfaces
- Pure LLM behavior research
- Pure ML architecture novelty without sensing application
- Theory-heavy ML (proofs, convergence theorems)
- Projects requiring expensive GPUs or cloud credits
- Projects requiring paid APIs
- Projects requiring formal participant studies or IRB approval
- Papers where the only novelty is "we used ChatGPT for X"
- Benchmark-chasing on ImageNet / CIFAR / standard NLP datasets

---

## Topic Selection Criteria

A good recommendation should score highly on all of:

- Feasibility in 8–12 weeks with student resources
- Low or zero cost
- Availability of public data or realistic data collection path
- Underexplored geography, domain, or modality
- Clear evaluation metrics
- Publication potential (Q1–Q2 journal or competitive conference)
- Alignment with applied ML + sensing strengths
- Potential to contribute to a coherent long-term research identity

## The Key Question

For every paper: **does this have the same research shape as my strongest work,
even if the domain is completely different?**

If yes → recommend it.
If no → reject it, even if it sounds exciting.
